The problem and success criteria

How could we move faster
without losing product judgement?

Success meant

Better-grounded decisions, fewer repeated corrections, and faster progress from hypothesis to a buildable solution with engineering.

The scope grew

What started as my own thought partner became a shared team system and reusable starter kit.

01

Product could become a bottleneck. As building accelerated, I wanted to work faster with engineering on the highest-value customer problems.

02

The context was scattered. Customer insights and company knowledge weren’t consistently informing key decisions.

03

AI kept repeating itself. Familiar assumptions and agreeable answers limited its value as a thought partner.

How I built and iterated on the second brain

Five iterations. One evolving system.

I treated my own workflow as a product: notice friction, change the system, observe what improved.

01

Ground it

“It doesn’t have all the context.”

I connected skills to customer interviews, support and sales feedback, product metrics, and domain knowledge.

What changedMore robust responses—and a thought partner I could trust more.
02

Help it learn

“I’ve corrected this before.”

I added auto-captured learnings, human review, memory updates, skill refinement, and recurring audits.

What changedFewer repeated assumptions and more productive strategic sparring.
03

Automate selectively

“I’m reviewing the same things.”

After a couple of months, I authorized auto-approval for recurring, less consequential themes with 100% manual approval over at least a month.

What changedLess time reviewing routine learnings, with more attention on those that needed judgement.
04

Share the benefit

“This only gets smarter for me.”

The GitHub structure helped me package a starter kit and shared team knowledge base. The product team adopted variations; engineering and other teams adapted parts of it.

What changedThe immediate team could work from shared context and compound learnings.
05

Make it simpler

“More skills isn’t always better.”

I reviewed usage and useful outcomes, consolidated overlapping skills, and removed those that added little value.

What changed18 skills became about 10 core skills for me and my immediate team.

01 / Auto-capture

Notice what is worth remembering.

Each session automatically captures proposed insights: corrections, decisions and their reasoning, recurring assumptions, and relevant constraints. I can also invoke learning capture mid-session.

How learnings are routed

Green · Auto-approve

Only within recurring, less consequential themes I explicitly authorized.

Yellow · Manual review

Likely approval, with my review required before applying the learning.

Red · Closer review

Medium-to-low confidence. Examine the insight and its evidence more closely.

How I made themes eligible for automation ＋

I started with manual review. After a couple of months, I authorized auto-approval for selected recurring, less consequential themes with a 100% manual approval rate over at least one month.

Claude’s confidence indicator considered that approval history, the consequence and potential impact of an incorrect approval, and the effort required to review the theme’s learnings. Review effort helped prioritize attention; historical approval was the benchmark, not a measure of accuracy.

I continued to approve, edit, reject, or defer learnings that required manual review.

A real example of the Second Brain in actionCredentialing growth & monetization at Thinkific

Solve the core need.
Build the path to growth.

My AI sparring skill challenged the investment case against customer, market, and product evidence. I used those challenges to define the strategy and gain leadership alignment.

The customer problem

Professional academies needed a reliable way to renew learner certifications. Without native recertification, customers duplicated courses as a workaround. The product question was how to close that gap while creating a sustainable path to growth.

The initial direction

Launch credentialing with usage caps and paid gates.

I initially proposed usage caps and paid gates across credentialing. The CEO and CRO also favoured making recertification exclusive to Plus at launch.

Core workflow→Paid gate

What the sparring skill brought into focus

  • ↘
    A smaller opportunity

    A flawed customer proxy overstated the relevant assessment cohort: 500+ customers became approximately 100 after validation.

  • ↔
    A product gap, with a workaround

    Customer, sales, support, and competitive evidence framed core recertification as an expected capability.

  • ↑
    Significant upgrade friction

    Many target customers were on self-serve plans. The substantial upgrade cost, combined with existing workarounds, weakened the case that core recertification would drive Plus upgrades.

The strategy I defined

Establish value in self-serve.
Monetize scale and complexity.

Define the path from self-serve adoption to sales-led expansion.

I broadened the investment thesis beyond assessment switching revenue to retention, acquisition, and Plus differentiation. The strategy connected cohesive professional workflows with richer in-platform data, useful customer insights, and monetization as customers scale.

Customer validation and ROI analysis then helped me align the CEO and CRO on uncapped core recertification from Grow upward. That decision freed pricing and packaging work, which I reprioritized toward API monetization.

How the evidence shaped the longer-term strategy ＋

The initial AI-assisted analysis, using another team’s deep-data input, estimated that 500+ customers used Brillium for assessment features missing from Thinkific. Its proxy included non-paying and churned customers. My Second Brain surfaced the data gap; validation put the relevant cohort closer to 100, approximately 80% smaller. This corrected a customer cohort, not the entire credentialing market.

The original case focused on customers consolidating assessment spend onto our platform through an add-on. I broadened the thesis to retention, sales acquisition, and Plus differentiation alongside switching revenue. I defined a direction that brought end-to-end professional credentialing workflows together, saved customers time, and retained more data in-platform to understand their needs and surface useful learner and program insights.

I interpreted the findings and set the growth and monetization direction. Subsequent customer validation and ROI analysis informed the uncapped recertification recommendation and executive alignment, freeing 4+ engineer-weeks for API monetization.

Delivered insights and the next steps ＋

Working with Analytics, I defined the customer segments, jobs, requirements, prototype, and success measures for the advanced dashboard; Analytics implemented it. The dashboard already consumed credentialing data and surfaced program health, recertification, and recurring-revenue opportunities to help professional customers shape their programs and businesses.

A contextual analytics link shipped through my connected agent workflow. Click-through among certification users with dashboard access rose from 10% to 35% over two weeks. Separately, new active certification programs created grew approximately 10% month over month. These have different measurement windows; the observations do not establish that the link caused all program growth or increased customer revenue.
